Illuminations / Sacred Paper

Artist’s Statement

← Gallery

I make minutely hand-cut paper collages that read as illuminated pages from books that do not exist—part sacred text, part manual of living. Working from diverse printed materials, I cut and place thousands of tiny paper pieces to illuminate grief, desire, literature and history as objects of ceremony and study.

Over six years I have composed 25 works—each complete in itself, yet together reading as an unbound manuscript for the education of enlightened womanhood, my revision of classical Western scholarly education. For instance, the study of history requires knowledge of The Demon of History and history as the doomed search for an origin. Differently, since enlightened womanhood requires engagement with the real world, works such as The Artist at Home and Conjugal Felicity contain accounts of and advice for daily living. Each work layers interdependent codes, allusions, and patterns to show its subject as a complex system of elliptical discourses.

With rich borders, esoteric marginalia, and textual annotations, my collages clearly reference illuminated medieval manuscripts. Those lavish books were not personal documents but attempts to stabilize wisdom as something absolute. My works, by contrast, are wisdom books for my own landscape, assembled from the physical remnants of the symbols and tropes that shape my everyday life. Their density reflects the challenge of transforming this abundance of personal references into meaningful wisdom. The collected papers are tangible records of lived moments; like meaning, they survive as explosions of fragments. By foregrounding the cut edges of paper, my collages reveal an effort to organize these fragments into a more enduring wisdom book.

Many of my motifs reflect my lifetime experience of crafts, humanistic education, and feelings of dread. Threads and looms, as in Corps Féminine and Arachne, suggest the paradox between female lived knowledge and abstraction. Gates and doors, like those in The Ethiopian Gate and both Dream works, use classical symbols to weigh the various costs of closure. By cutting apart canonical stories and works and re-stitching them, I comment on the original symbols and their rhetoric.

Like all paper, my works are meant to be read and doubted.

Expanded Bio

Trudi Connolly is a Tucson-based visual artist working primarily in hand-cut paper collage. Born in Tucson, she has lived and studied in Sweden, France, China, New York, and California. She completed a BA/MA in Comparative Literature at New York University (French and Critical Theory), with additional studies in Politics and Sociology; she later taught English in China and pursued doctoral study in literature at the University of California, Irvine. Drawing on this humanistic and social-scientific training, Connolly assembles fragments of printed culture—manuscripts, marginalia, and vernacular ephemera—into manuscript-like works that examine history, domestic life, desire, and the fragile ways we hold knowledge. Cutting and re-stitching text and image, she composes speculative scriptures. A six-year cycle of 25 collages can be encountered individually or together, much like an unbound book.
